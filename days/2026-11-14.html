Question 1

Maths, Parabola JEE Main 2025 (January session)

Let the shortest distance from $(a, 0)$, $a>0$, to the parabola $y^2=4x$ be $4$. Then the equation of the circle passing through the point $(a, 0)$ and the focus of the parabola, and having its centre on the axis of the parabola, is:

  1. A$x^2+y^2-10x+9=0$
  2. B$x^2+y^2-6x+5=0$
  3. C$x^2+y^2-4x+3=0$
  4. D$x^2+y^2-8x+7=0$
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (B) $x^2+y^2-6x+5=0$

Find $a$ by minimising the distance from $(a,0)$ to a general point on the parabola. Then find the circle through two points on the $x$-axis.

  1. Any point on $y^2=4x$ can be written as $(t^2, 2t)$. Its squared distance from $(a,0)$ is:

    $$D=(t^2-a)^2+4t^2$$

  2. Put $u=t^2$, where $u\ge 0$. Then:

    $$D=(u-a)^2+4u$$

  3. Minimise by setting the derivative to zero:

    $$2(u-a)+4=0$$
    $$u=a-2$$

  4. If $a>2$, this value of $u$ is allowed. Substitute it back:

    $$\begin{aligned} D_{\min} &= (-2)^2+4(a-2) \\[6pt] &= 4a-4 \end{aligned}$$

  5. The shortest distance is $4$, so $D_{\min}=16$:

    $$4a-4=16 \;\Rightarrow\; a=5$$

    This satisfies $a>2$. If $a\le 2$, the minimum would be at $u=0$, giving distance $a=4$. That contradicts $a\le 2$, so $a=5$ is the only possibility.

  6. The focus of $y^2=4x$ is $(1,0)$, and its axis is the $x$-axis. Let the centre be $(h,0)$. It is the same distance from $(1,0)$ and $(5,0)$, so:

    $$h=\dfrac{1+5}{2}=3$$

    The radius is $5-3=2$.

  7. The equation of the circle is:

    $$\begin{aligned} (x-3)^2+y^2 &= 4 \\[6pt] x^2+y^2-6x+5 &= 0 \end{aligned}$$

    This matches option (B).

Question 2

Maths, Circle JEE Main 2024 (April session)

Let the circles $C_1:(x-\alpha)^2+(y-\beta)^2=r_1^2$ and $C_2:(x-8)^2+\left(y-\dfrac{15}{2}\right)^2=r_2^2$ touch each other externally at the point $(6,6)$. If the point $(6,6)$ divides the line segment joining the centres of $C_1$ and $C_2$ internally in the ratio $2:1$, find the value of $(\alpha+\beta)+4\left(r_1^2+r_2^2\right)$.

  1. A125
  2. B130
  3. C110
  4. D145
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (B) 130

Use the section formula to find the centre of $C_1$. The radius of each circle is then its centre's distance to the point where the circles touch, $(6,6)$.

  1. The centres are $(\alpha,\beta)$ and $\left(8,\dfrac{15}{2}\right)$. The point $(6,6)$ divides the segment joining them in the ratio $2:1$, so:

    $$x=\dfrac{1\cdot\alpha+2\cdot 8}{3}=6$$
    $$y=\dfrac{1\cdot\beta+2\cdot\frac{15}{2}}{3}=6$$

  2. Solve these two equations for $\alpha$ and $\beta$:

    $$\begin{aligned} \alpha+16 &= 18 \\[6pt] \alpha &= 2 \end{aligned}$$
    $$\begin{aligned} \beta+15 &= 18 \\[6pt] \beta &= 3 \end{aligned}$$

    So $\alpha+\beta=5$.

  3. The circles touch at $(6,6)$, so $r_1$ is the distance from $(2,3)$ to $(6,6)$:

    $$\begin{aligned} r_1^2 &= (6-2)^2+(6-3)^2 \\[6pt] &= 16+9 \\[6pt] &= 25 \end{aligned}$$

  4. In the same way, $r_2$ is the distance from $\left(8,\dfrac{15}{2}\right)$ to $(6,6)$:

    $$\begin{aligned} r_2^2 &= (8-6)^2+\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2 \\[6pt] &= 4+\dfrac{9}{4} \\[6pt] &= \dfrac{25}{4} \end{aligned}$$

  5. Put these values into the expression:

    $$\begin{aligned} 4(r_1^2+r_2^2) &= 4\left(25+\dfrac{25}{4}\right) \\[6pt] &= 100+25 \\[6pt] &= 125 \end{aligned}$$
    $$\begin{aligned} (\alpha+\beta)+125 &= 5+125 \\[6pt] &= 130 \end{aligned}$$
