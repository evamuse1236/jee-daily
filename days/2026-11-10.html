Question 1

Physics, Work power energy JEE Main 2025 (January session)

Given below are two statements: one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R.

Assertion A: In a central force field, the work done is independent of the path chosen.

Reason R: Every force encountered in mechanics does not have an associated potential energy.

In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below.

  1. AA is false but R is true
  2. BBoth A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. CA is true but R is false
  4. DBoth A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (B) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A

Check each statement separately. Then ask whether R is the reason A is true.

  1. In a central force field, the force always points along the line to a fixed centre. Its size depends only on the distance $r$ from that centre:

    $$\vec F = F(r)\,\hat r$$

  2. Only the movement along $\hat r$ does work. Any sideways movement, at right angles to $\hat r$, does no work:

    $$\begin{aligned} W &= \int \vec F\cdot d\vec s \\[6pt] &= \int_{r_1}^{r_2} F(r)\,dr \end{aligned}$$

    The work depends only on the start and end distances $r_1$ and $r_2$. It does not depend on the path, so A is true. This means a central force is conservative, like gravity or the electric force.

  3. R says that not every force in mechanics has a potential energy. That is correct. Friction and air drag are non-conservative forces: the work they do depends on the path, so they have no potential energy. So R is true.

  4. R is about forces that are not conservative. A is about central forces, which are conservative. R says nothing about why the work done by a central force does not depend on the path. So R does not explain A.

Question 2

Physics, Work power energy JEE Main 2025 (April session)

A block of mass $1\ \text{kg}$ is moving along the $x$-axis with speed $v_i = 10\ \text{m/s}$. It enters a rough region that runs from $x = 0.1\ \text{m}$ to $x = 1.9\ \text{m}$. In this range, the retarding force on the block is $F_r = -kx\ \text{N}$, with $k = 10\ \text{N/m}$. What is the final speed of the block as it leaves the rough region?

  1. A$10\ \text{m/s}$
  2. B$4\ \text{m/s}$
  3. C$6\ \text{m/s}$
  4. D$8\ \text{m/s}$
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (D) $8\ \text{m/s}$

Use the work–energy theorem: the work done by the retarding force equals the change in kinetic energy.

  1. Find the starting kinetic energy.

    $$K_i = \dfrac{1}{2}mv_i^2$$
    $$\begin{aligned} K_i &= \dfrac{1}{2}(1)(10)^2 \\[6pt] &= 50\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

  2. The force changes with $x$, so find the work done by integrating it from $x = 0.1$ to $x = 1.9$.

    $$W = \int_{0.1}^{1.9} -10x\,dx$$
    $$\begin{aligned} W &= -5\left(1.9^2 - 0.1^2\right) \\[6pt] &= -5(3.61 - 0.01) \\[6pt] &= -18\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

  3. Add this work to the starting kinetic energy to get the final kinetic energy.

    $$\begin{aligned} K_f &= 50 - 18 \\[6pt] &= 32\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

  4. Use the final kinetic energy to find the final speed.

    $$\dfrac{1}{2}(1)v_f^2 = 32$$
    $$\begin{aligned} v_f^2 &= 64 \\[6pt] v_f &= 8\ \text{m/s} \end{aligned}$$
