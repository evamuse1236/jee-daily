Question 1

Maths, Parabola JEE Main 2019 (January session)

The vertex of the parabola $y^{2}+4\left(x-a^{2}\right)=0$ is one vertex of a triangle. The other two vertices are the points where the parabola meets the $y$-axis. If the area of this triangle is 250 sq. units, then a value of $a$ is:

  1. A$5\sqrt{5}$
  2. B$5\left(2^{1/3}\right)$
  3. C$(10)^{33}$
  4. D$5$
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (D) $5$

Find the three corner points, then use area = ½ × base × height and solve for $a$.

  1. Rewrite the parabola in standard form. Its vertex is where $y = 0$ and $x = a^2$.

    $$y^2 = -4\left(x - a^2\right)$$
    $$\text{Vertex} = (a^2,\ 0)$$

  2. Find where the parabola meets the $y$-axis by putting $x = 0$.

    $$\begin{aligned} y^2 &= 4a^2 \\[6pt] y &= \pm 2a \end{aligned}$$

    So the points are $(0,\ 2a)$ and $(0,\ -2a)$.

  3. The base is the segment on the $y$-axis between these two points. Its length is $4|a|$.

  4. The height is the distance from the vertex $(a^2, 0)$ to the $y$-axis, which is $a^2$.

  5. Write the area and set it equal to 250.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area} &= \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 4|a| \cdot a^2 \\[6pt] &= 2|a|^3 \\[6pt] &= 250 \end{aligned}$$

  6. Solve for $a$.

    $$\begin{aligned} |a|^3 &= 125 \\[6pt] |a| &= 5 \end{aligned}$$

    So $a = 5$ is a valid value, which is option (D).

Question 2

Physics, Work power energy JEE Main 2025 (January session)

A force $F = \alpha + \beta x^2$ acts on an object in the $x$-direction. The work done by the force is $5\ \text{J}$ when the object is displaced by $1\ \text{m}$. If the constant $\alpha = 1\ \text{N}$, then $\beta$ will be

  1. A$15\ \text{N/m}^2$
  2. B$12\ \text{N/m}^2$
  3. C$8\ \text{N/m}^2$
  4. D$10\ \text{N/m}^2$
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (B) $12\ \text{N/m}^2$

The force changes with position, so the work done is the integral of the force over the displacement.

  1. Work done by a force that changes with position is found by integrating it from $x = 0$ to $x = 1$.

    $$W = \int_0^1 F\,dx$$

  2. Put in the given force and integrate each term.

    $$\begin{aligned} W &= \int_0^1 (\alpha + \beta x^2)\,dx \\[6pt] &= \alpha x + \dfrac{\beta x^3}{3}\Big|_0^1 \\[6pt] &= \alpha + \dfrac{\beta}{3} \end{aligned}$$

  3. Use $W = 5\ \text{J}$ and $\alpha = 1\ \text{N}$.

    $$\begin{aligned} 5 &= 1 + \dfrac{\beta}{3} \\[6pt] \dfrac{\beta}{3} &= 4 \end{aligned}$$

  4. Solve for $\beta$.

    $$\beta = 12\ \text{N/m}^2$$

Question 1

Physics, Work power energy JEE Main 2025 (January session)

Given below are two statements: one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R.

Assertion A: In a central force field, the work done is independent of the path chosen.

Reason R: Every force encountered in mechanics does not have an associated potential energy.

In the light of the above statements, choose the most appropriate answer from the options given below.

  1. AA is false but R is true
  2. BBoth A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
  3. CA is true but R is false
  4. DBoth A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (B) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A

Check each statement separately. Then ask whether R is the reason A is true.

  1. In a central force field, the force always points along the line to a fixed centre. Its size depends only on the distance $r$ from that centre:

    $$\vec F = F(r)\,\hat r$$

  2. Only the movement along $\hat r$ does work. Any sideways movement, at right angles to $\hat r$, does no work:

    $$\begin{aligned} W &= \int \vec F\cdot d\vec s \\[6pt] &= \int_{r_1}^{r_2} F(r)\,dr \end{aligned}$$

    The work depends only on the start and end distances $r_1$ and $r_2$. It does not depend on the path, so A is true. This means a central force is conservative, like gravity or the electric force.

  3. R says that not every force in mechanics has a potential energy. That is correct. Friction and air drag are non-conservative forces: the work they do depends on the path, so they have no potential energy. So R is true.

  4. R is about forces that are not conservative. A is about central forces, which are conservative. R says nothing about why the work done by a central force does not depend on the path. So R does not explain A.

Question 2

Physics, Work power energy JEE Main 2025 (April session)

A block of mass $1\ \text{kg}$ is moving along the $x$-axis with speed $v_i = 10\ \text{m/s}$. It enters a rough region that runs from $x = 0.1\ \text{m}$ to $x = 1.9\ \text{m}$. In this range, the retarding force on the block is $F_r = -kx\ \text{N}$, with $k = 10\ \text{N/m}$. What is the final speed of the block as it leaves the rough region?

  1. A$10\ \text{m/s}$
  2. B$4\ \text{m/s}$
  3. C$6\ \text{m/s}$
  4. D$8\ \text{m/s}$
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (D) $8\ \text{m/s}$

Use the work–energy theorem: the work done by the retarding force equals the change in kinetic energy.

  1. Find the starting kinetic energy.

    $$K_i = \dfrac{1}{2}mv_i^2$$
    $$\begin{aligned} K_i &= \dfrac{1}{2}(1)(10)^2 \\[6pt] &= 50\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

  2. The force changes with $x$, so find the work done by integrating it from $x = 0.1$ to $x = 1.9$.

    $$W = \int_{0.1}^{1.9} -10x\,dx$$
    $$\begin{aligned} W &= -5\left(1.9^2 - 0.1^2\right) \\[6pt] &= -5(3.61 - 0.01) \\[6pt] &= -18\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

  3. Add this work to the starting kinetic energy to get the final kinetic energy.

    $$\begin{aligned} K_f &= 50 - 18 \\[6pt] &= 32\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

  4. Use the final kinetic energy to find the final speed.

    $$\dfrac{1}{2}(1)v_f^2 = 32$$
    $$\begin{aligned} v_f^2 &= 64 \\[6pt] v_f &= 8\ \text{m/s} \end{aligned}$$

Question 1

Physics, Laws of motion JEE Main 2025 (January session)

A massless spring gets elongated by amount $x_1$ under a tension of $5\ \text{N}$. Its elongation is $x_2$ under a tension of $7\ \text{N}$. For an elongation of $\left(5x_1 - 2x_2\right)$, what will the tension in the spring be?

  1. A$39\ \text{N}$
  2. B$15\ \text{N}$
  3. C$11\ \text{N}$
  4. D$20\ \text{N}$
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (C) $11\ \text{N}$

Use Hooke's law, which says the tension in a spring equals its stiffness times its stretch: $T = kx$.

  1. Write Hooke's law for the first case, where the tension is $5\ \text{N}$.

    $$kx_1 = 5$$

  2. Write it for the second case, where the tension is $7\ \text{N}$.

    $$kx_2 = 7$$

  3. For the new stretch $(5x_1 - 2x_2)$, the tension is $k$ times that stretch.

    $$\begin{aligned} T &= k(5x_1 - 2x_2) \\[6pt] &= 5(kx_1) - 2(kx_2) \end{aligned}$$

  4. Put in the values from Steps 1 and 2.

    $$\begin{aligned} T &= 5(5) - 2(7) \\[6pt] &= 25 - 14 \\[6pt] &= 11\ \text{N} \end{aligned}$$

Question 2

Maths, Quadratic equation JEE Main 2025 (January session)

If the set of all $a \in \mathbb{R}$ for which the equation $2x^2+(a-5)x+15=3a$ has no real root is the interval $(\alpha, \beta)$, and $X=\{x \in \mathbb{Z} : \alpha < x < \beta\}$, then $\displaystyle\sum_{x \in X} x^2$ is equal to:

  1. A2109
  2. B2129
  3. C2119
  4. D2139
Show solutionHide solution

Answer: (D) 2139

A quadratic has no real roots exactly when its discriminant is negative. This condition gives the interval for $a$. Then we add the squares of the integers inside that interval.

  1. Move every term to one side so the equation is in standard form.

    $$2x^2+(a-5)x+(15-3a)=0$$

  2. Write the discriminant $D=b^2-4ac$.

    $$\begin{aligned} D &= (a-5)^2-8(15-3a) \\[6pt] &= a^2-10a+25-120+24a \\[6pt] &= a^2+14a-95 \end{aligned}$$

  3. For no real root we need $D<0$. Find where $a^2+14a-95=0$.

    $$\begin{aligned} a &= -7 \pm \sqrt{49+95} \\[6pt] &= -7 \pm 12 \end{aligned}$$

    So $a=-19$ or $a=5$.

  4. The expression $a^2+14a-95$ is negative between its two roots. So $D<0$ when $-19<a<5$, which gives $\alpha=-19$ and $\beta=5$.

  5. The integers strictly between $-19$ and $5$ are $-18, -17, \dots, 4$. A negative number has the same square as its positive partner, so the sum of the squares is

    $$(1^2+\dots+18^2)+0^2+(1^2+\dots+4^2)$$

  6. Use the formula $1^2+2^2+\dots+n^2 = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$.

    $$\begin{aligned} \sum_{1}^{18} k^2 &= \dfrac{18\cdot19\cdot37}{6} \\[6pt] &= 2109 \end{aligned}$$
    $$1^2+2^2+3^2+4^2=30$$

  7. Add the two parts.

    $$2109+30=2139$$
